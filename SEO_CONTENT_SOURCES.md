# MotorBrief SEO content — verified sources and data boundaries

Checked 3 November 2026. These notes support public informational pages; they do not expand MotorBrief's connected vehicle-data products.

## UK government and consumer sources

- GOV.UK MOT-history service: https://www.gov.uk/check-mot-history — lists past MOT pass/fail results, mileage recorded at tests and the next MOT due date. For cars, motorcycles and vans, the GOV.UK service lists tests in England, Scotland and Wales since 2005, and tests in Northern Ireland since 2017. MotorBrief must still qualify its own coverage as dependent on UKVD's response for the vehicle.
- GOV.UK vehicle-tax checker: https://www.gov.uk/check-vehicle-tax — allows a registration-based check for up-to-date tax or SORN; tax-rate calculation may require the 11-digit V5C reference. MotorBrief may display only fields actually returned by its current provider response.
- GOV.UK used-car checks: https://www.gov.uk/checks-when-buying-a-used-car — recommends comparing seller-supplied registration, make/model and MOT-test information against DVLA data, checking MOT history and recalls, and inspecting the V5C, vehicle identification number and engine number in person.
- GOV.UK insurance write-offs: https://www.gov.uk/scrapped-and-written-off-vehicles/insurance-writeoffs — Category A and B vehicles cannot be repaired; A vehicles must be crushed, while a B vehicle's body shell must be crushed and other parts may be salvaged. Category N is repairable after non-structural damage; Category S is repairable after structural damage. The vehicle may be used again if repaired to a roadworthy condition. This is general guidance only; MotorBrief does not currently request a write-off data package.
- Citizens Advice, England-specific hire-purchase guidance: https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/consumer/buying-or-repairing-a-car/the-car-you-bought-is-still-on-hire-purchase/ — title/ownership outcomes can depend on the facts, including good faith and whether the buyer is a private buyer; Citizens Advice has separate pages for Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland. Do not turn this into an absolute legal claim. MotorBrief does not currently query finance data.

## Market data source

- SMMT, Q2 2026 used-car market release: https://www.smmt.co.uk/used-car-market-returns-to-growth-as-ev-sales-accelerate/ — confirms 2,009,318 UK used-car transactions in Q2 2026, up 0.7%; battery-electric used-car transactions rose 67.0% to 110,761. SMMT notes its data is based on information available at publication and may be revised by DVLA. Any homepage chart using the Q2 2026 values must show the period and source and must not imply that these are live or current-month data.

## MotorBrief's connected data boundary

Project source checked 3 November 2026: `server/report.js` requests only UKVD `VehicleData` and `MotHistoryData`; `server/index.js` performs an explicit `VehicleData` request for the free-to-visitor registration check. Displayed details must be limited to provider-returned make/model/colour/fuel/year, MOT/tax fields where present, returned MOT tests and mileage readings, and keeper information only where found in the API response. Coverage and data availability vary by vehicle/account response.

The current server does not call a finance/VDI package, write-off package, theft check, damage/repair package, service-history package, advertisement/photo package, recall/NCAP package or a maintained model-level EV technical-specification source. Public copy may explain these topics as buyer guidance, but must say they are not included in MotorBrief's current live lookup/report. The EV name suggestions are not a specifications feed, and the £4.99 Essential EV Checkout remains test-only; do not advertise it as a live purchasable report.


## Long-form used-car buyer article sources (checked 4 November 2026)

- GOV.UK, “Check a used vehicle you’re buying”: https://www.gov.uk/checks-when-buying-a-used-car — official guidance asks buyers to obtain the registration, make, model and MOT test number; compare supplied details with records; check MOT history and recalls; inspect the V5C, its security details, and the vehicle and engine identification numbers. It explicitly frames these steps as helping check that the vehicle is not stolen or unsafe. Do not claim MotorBrief independently checks police theft databases.
- GOV.UK, “Check the MOT history of a vehicle”: https://www.gov.uk/check-mot-history — official results include pass/fail, recorded mileage and next MOT due date. In England, Scotland and Wales, cars/motorcycles/vans are available from 2005; Northern Ireland results are available from 2017. The service may also show test defects and recalls depending on country/manufacturer.
- GOV.UK, “Check if a vehicle is taxed”: https://www.gov.uk/check-vehicle-tax — registration-based tax/SORN status; approved tax or SORN updates can take up to two working days; checking current rates may need the 11-digit V5C reference.
- GOV.UK, “Insurance write-offs”: https://www.gov.uk/scrapped-and-written-off-vehicles/insurance-writeoffs — Category A cannot be repaired and must be crushed; Category B cannot be repaired and the shell must be crushed, while parts may be salvaged; Category N can be repaired after non-structural damage; Category S can be repaired after structural damage. N/S vehicles can be used again if repaired to roadworthy condition. MotorBrief does not request a write-off package.
- Citizens Advice, “If the car you bought is still on hire purchase”: https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/consumer/buying-or-repairing-a-car/the-car-you-bought-is-still-on-hire-purchase/ — page applies to England and explains that title can depend on circumstances including the buyer's good faith, private-buyer status and whether they are the first private buyer. Separate regional pages exist. Avoid a universal legal outcome; recommend jurisdiction-specific advice. MotorBrief does not request finance data.

The owner-supplied target search phrases for the article are: “UK vehicle history check” (primary), “used car history check”, “car registration check”, “MOT history check”, “outstanding car finance check”, “write-off history check”, “stolen vehicle check” and “mileage discrepancy check”. Use them naturally as buyer-intent phrases, not as claims that MotorBrief supplies every check. The current product only displays fields returned by its connected third-party vehicle/MOT requests. Recommend the GOV.UK buyer checklist for stolen-logbook and identity/document checks; do not claim that MotorBrief searches theft databases.
